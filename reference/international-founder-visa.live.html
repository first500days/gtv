Frequently asked

Answers before you apply.

I've already been refused once. Can you still help?
Often, yes — and it's a large share of the work we do. A refusal isn't fatal, but a second application that repeats the first one's weaknesses usually is. We start by reading the refusal properly: what the assessor actually said, what they meant, and which of it is fixable. Sometimes the answer is a rebuild and a resubmission. Sometimes it's a different route entirely. Occasionally it's that you shouldn't reapply, and we'll say that.
Are you immigration advisers?
No, and we're careful about the distinction. We build the business, the evidence and the endorsement case. Regulated immigration counsel handles the legal filing and any advice about your immigration status — either your own solicitor or one we introduce you to. Anyone offering to do both under one roof, without regulation, is a risk to you.
Do you guarantee endorsement or a visa?
No credible partner should, and in several jurisdictions promising an outcome is itself a regulatory problem. What we commit to is an endorsement-ready venture and a case built to the assessing body's published criteria. The decision belongs to the endorsing body and the immigration authority.
How much money do I need behind the business?
It depends entirely on the route, and the requirements have changed more than once in recent years — several of the numbers still circulating online are out of date. We give you the current position for your route in the first week, from the official source, in writing.
Can my family come with me?
Most founder routes allow dependants — partner and children — with their own fees, evidence and maintenance requirements. It affects your budget and your timeline meaningfully, so we scope it in the diagnostic rather than leaving it to be discovered later.
What happens if we're refused after all this work?
We tell you where it failed, and we stay to fix it. You keep everything built — the company, the IP, the product, the customers — because it was built as a real business rather than as an exhibit. Depending on the reason, the options are a re-submission with new evidence, a different endorsing body, or a different route. We don't refund on outcome, and we say so up front: our fee buys the build, not the decision.
How long does the whole thing take?
Most founders reach endorsement-ready in 8–12 weeks from a standing start. Ventures that need real market evidence built — most of them — run to six months before the case is strong. After that, assessment and immigration processing run on the authority's timetable, not ours. We then stay 12 months or more into operating.
Which route is right for me?
That's the first question the diagnostic answers, and it's rarely the route people arrive asking about. Sector, stage, capital, family situation, settlement timeline and how much of your business can move all change the answer. Choosing on familiarity rather than fit is the most common expensive mistake in this market.
I already have a running business. Does that help?
It helps with viability and hurts nothing — but an existing business abroad is not automatically an endorsable one. Assessors look for innovation and for a genuine commitment to building in their market. We usually shape a distinct venture or a defensible new line rather than presenting the existing company unchanged.
Who actually does the work?
A named operator leads your engagement, supported by our product, legal and growth teams across Gurugram, London, Ajman and Singapore. You'll meet them on the diagnostic call, not after signing.
What does it cost, all in?
Our fees run £1,500 to £10,000+ across three tiers, plus an optional retainer. Government, endorsement, legal and incorporation costs sit outside that and are listed in full above. Every fee is credited if we can't agree a route and a plan by the end of week two.
